How to work out your real profit per Shopee order

Your sales dashboard shows revenue. It doesn't show what's left after fees, vouchers, packaging and ads. Here's how to find the number that actually matters.

Why sales aren't profit

A ₱500 order feels like ₱500. But before that money is yours, the platform takes its commission and transaction fee, you may have paid for a voucher or free shipping, and the item, bubble wrap and box all cost something. Many sellers find out their best-selling product barely breaks even only when they finally add it up.

The formula

Profit per order = Selling price − Platform fees − Product cost − Packaging − Your share of shipping − Vouchers you fund − Ad cost per order

  • Platform fees: commission plus transaction or payment fees. The exact percentages depend on your platform, category and programmes you've joined (free shipping, cashback). Check the current rates in your seller centre; they change.
  • Product cost: what one unit cost you delivered to your door, including shipping from the supplier.
  • Packaging: pouch, box, tape, thank-you card. Count it. ₱8–15 per order adds up.
  • Vouchers you fund: shop vouchers come out of your money; platform-funded ones usually don't.
  • Ad cost per order: last month's ad spend divided by orders that came from ads.

Worked example

These numbers are an example only. Put in your own fee rates.

ItemAmount
Selling price₱499
Platform fees (example: 12% of price)−₱59.88
Product cost, landed−₱180
Packaging−₱12
Shop voucher (₱20 off)−₱20
Ads (₱1,500 spend ÷ 60 ad orders)−₱25
Profit per order₱202.12 (about 40%)

Now try the same product at ₱399 with the same costs: fees become ₱47.88 and profit drops to ₱114.12, about 29%. A ₱100 price cut took away more than 40% of the profit. That's why it's worth doing this sum before every sale event.

Three habits that protect your margin

  1. Work out profit per product, not per month. A good month can hide one product that loses money on every order.
  2. Set a floor price. Decide the lowest price you'll accept for each product before a mega sale, not during it.
  3. Re-check after fee changes. When your platform announces new rates, update your numbers the same week.

Do it automatically

Doing this by hand for every order gets old fast. A spreadsheet that takes your order list and does the maths for you saves hours a month and shows which products are worth keeping.
