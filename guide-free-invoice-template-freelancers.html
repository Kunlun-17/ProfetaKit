Free invoice template for freelancers, and what every invoice needs

Late payments often start with a vague invoice. Here's what to put on yours so clients can pay without asking questions, plus a free template that does the maths.

The 9 things every freelance invoice needs

  1. The word "Invoice" and a unique number (INV-001, INV-002…). Accounts teams file by number.
  2. Issue date and due date. "Due in 14 days" is vaguer than a date. Write the date.
  3. Your name or business name, email and address.
  4. The client's company name and the person who approves payment.
  5. What you did, line by line. "Homepage design" and "Build, 12 hours at $40" are easier to approve than "Website work".
  6. Quantity, rate and amount for each line.
  7. Subtotal, any tax, and the total due.
  8. How to pay: bank details, PayPal or Wise, with the exact account name.
  9. A one-line note: "Thank you! Payment is due by 14 March."

How to get paid faster

  • Send it the day the work is delivered. Every day you wait is a day added to when you get paid.
  • Ask for a deposit on new clients. 30–50% before you start is normal.
  • Send a friendly reminder three days before the due date. Most "late" payments are invoices someone forgot about.
  • Keep a list of what's unpaid. If you can't see who owes you, you can't chase it.
